# 兵令上 · Military Orders, Part I

## 1

**兵令上:** 兵者，凶器也。争者，逆德也。事必有本，故王者伐暴乱，本仁义焉。战国则以立威，抗敌，相图，不能废兵也。

War is a weapon of violence. Strife is contrary to virtue. Every matter has its foundation, so a sovereign ruler attacks violent and disorderly forces with the foundation of benevolence and righteousness. During the Warring States period, states relied on war to establish authority, resist enemies, and plot against each other; thus, they could not abandon military force.

## 2

**兵令上:** 兵者以武为植，以文为种。武为表，文为里。能审此二者，知胜败矣。文所以视利害，辨安危；武所以犯强敌，力攻守也。

Military power takes martial strength as its foundation and civil virtue as its seed. Martial strength is the outward manifestation, while civil virtue is the inner essence. One who can discern these two aspects understands victory and defeat. Civil virtue is used to assess benefits and harms, distinguish safety from danger; Martial strength is employed to confront powerful enemies and exert force in attack and defense.

## 3

**兵令上:** 专一则胜，离散则败。陈以密则固，锋以疏则达。卒畏将甚于敌者胜，卒畏敌甚于将者败。所以知胜败者，称将于敌也，敌与将犹权衡焉。安静则治，暴疾则乱。

Unity leads to victory, while disunity leads to defeat. A formation is solid when arranged closely; a blade strikes effectively when wielded with openness and flexibility. An army that fears its general more than the enemy will win, while an army that fears the enemy more than its general will lose. Therefore, one who understands victory and defeat compares the general to the enemy; the enemy and the general are like a balance scale. Calmness brings order, while violence and haste bring disorder.

## 4

**兵令上:** 出卒陈兵有常令，行伍疏数有常法，先后之次有适宜。常令者，非追北袭邑攸用也。前后不次则失也。乱先后斩之。

Deploying troops and arranging the army must follow established orders; the spacing of ranks and files must adhere to fixed regulations, and the order of precedence should be appropriate. Established orders are not used for chasing defeated enemies or raiding towns at will. If the order of front and rear is disarranged, it leads to failure. Those who disrupt the order of precedence shall be executed.

## 5

**兵令上:** 常陈皆向敌，有内向，有外向，有立陈，有坐陈。夫内向所以顾中也，外向所以备外也，立陈所以进也，坐陈所以止也，立坐之陈，相参进止，将在其中。坐之兵剑斧，立之兵戟弩，将亦居中。

Standard formations are all directed toward the enemy; they may face inward or outward, and include standing formations or sitting formations. An inward-facing formation is used to protect the center, an outward-facing one prepares for external threats; a standing formation signifies advancement, while a sitting formation indicates halting. The combination of standing and sitting formations allows coordinated movement and halting, with the general positioned among them. In a sitting formation, soldiers carry swords and axes; in a standing formation, they wield halberds and crossbows, with the general also positioned at the center.

## 6

**兵令上:** 善御敌者，正兵先合，而后振之，此必胜之术也。陈之斧钺，饰之旗章，有功必赏，犯令必死，存亡死生，在枹之端，虽天下有善兵者，莫能御此矣。

A skilled commander in repelling enemies first engages the main forces and then mobilizes reinforcements; this is the sure way to victory. The axes and halberds of the formation, adorned with banners and insignias, ensure rewards for those who achieve merit and death for those who violate orders. The survival or destruction, life or death, rests at the tip of the drumstick; even if there is a skilled general in the world, none can resist this force.

## 7

**兵令上:** 矢射未交，长刃未接，前噪者谓之虚，后噪者谓之实，不噪者谓之秘，虚实者兵之体也。

Before arrows are exchanged and long weapons clash, those who shout from the front are called \"xu\" (empty), those shouting from the rear are called \"shi\" (solid), and those who remain silent are called \"mi\" (secret). The concepts of xu and shi form the essence of military strategy.
