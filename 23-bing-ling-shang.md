# 兵令上 · Military Orders, Part I

## 1

**兵令上:** 兵者，凶器也。争者，逆德也。事必有本，故王者伐暴乱，本仁义焉。战国则以立威，抗敌，相图，不能废兵也。

War is a weapon of violence. Strife is contrary to virtue. Every matter has its foundation, so a sovereign ruler attacks violent and disorderly forces with the foundation of benevolence and righteousness. During the Warring States period, states relied on war to establish authority, resist enemies, and plot against each other; thus, they could not abandon military force.

## 2

**兵令上:** 兵者以武为植，以文为种。武为表，文为里。能审此二者，知胜败矣。文所以视利害，辨安危；武所以犯强敌，力攻守也。

Military power takes martial strength as its foundation and civil virtue as its seed. Martial strength is the outward manifestation, while civil virtue is the inner essence. One who can discern these two aspects understands victory and defeat. Civil virtue is used to assess benefits and harms, distinguish safety from danger; Martial strength is employed to confront powerful enemies and exert force in attack and defense.

## 3

**兵令上:** 专一则胜，离散则败。陈以密则固，锋以疏则达。卒畏将甚于敌者胜，卒畏敌甚于将者败。所以知胜败者，称将于敌也，敌与将犹权衡焉。安静则治，暴疾则乱。

Unity leads to victory, while disunity leads to defeat. A formation is solid when arranged closely; a blade strikes effectively when wielded with openness and flexibility. An army that fears its general more than the enemy will win, while an army that fears the enemy more than its general will lose. Therefore, one who understands victory and defeat compares the general to the enemy; the enemy and the general are like a balance scale. Calmness brings order, while violence and haste bring disorder.

## 4

**兵令上:** 出卒陈兵有常令，行伍疏数有常法，先后之次有适宜。常令者，非追北袭邑攸用也。前后不次则失也。乱先后斩之。

Deploying troops and arranging the army must follow established orders; the spacing of ranks and files must adhere to fixed regulations, and the order of precedence should be appropriate. Established orders are not used for chasing defeated enemies or raiding towns at will. If the order of front and rear is disarranged, it leads to failure. Those who disrupt the order of precedence shall be executed.

## 5

**兵令上:** 常陈皆向敌，有内向，有外向，有立陈，有坐陈。夫内向所以顾中也，外向所以备外也，立陈所以进也，坐陈所以止也，立坐之陈，相参进止，将在其中。坐之兵剑斧，立之兵戟弩，将亦居中。

Standard formations are all directed toward the enemy; they may face inward or outward, and include standing formations or sitting formations. An inward-facing formation is used to protect the center, an outward-facing one prepares for external threats; a standing formation signifies advancement, while a sitting formation indicates halting. The combination of standing and sitting formations allows coordinated movement and halting, with the general positioned among them. In a sitting formation, soldiers carry swords and axes; in a standing formation, they wield halberds and crossbows, with the general also positioned at the center.

## 6

**兵令上:** 善御敌者，正兵先合，而后振之，此必胜之术也。陈之斧钺，饰之旗章，有功必赏，犯令必死，存亡死生，在枹之端，虽天下有善兵者，莫能御此矣。

A skilled commander in repelling enemies first engages the main forces and then mobilizes reinforcements; this is the sure way to victory. The axes and halberds of the formation, adorned with banners and insignias, ensure rewards for those who achieve merit and death for those who violate orders. The survival or destruction, life or death, rests at the tip of the drumstick; even if there is a skilled general in the world, none can resist this force.

## 7

**兵令上:** 矢射未交，长刃未接，前噪者谓之虚，后噪者谓之实，不噪者谓之秘，虚实者兵之体也。

Before arrows are exchanged and long weapons clash, those who shout from the front are called \"xu\" (empty), those shouting from the rear are called \"shi\" (solid), and those who remain silent are called \"mi\" (secret). The concepts of xu and shi form the essence of military strategy.

## 📖 银雀山汉简异文

1972年山东临沂银雀山一号汉墓出土的竹简中有〈兵令〉篇，篇幅相对完整，对应今本《兵令上》《兵令下》两篇。汉简开篇作"兵者，凶器、逆德；争者，事之末也"，保存了今本删去的对战争的"末事"定位：

**银雀山汉简〈兵令〉释文（对应《兵令上》部分）：** 兵者，凶器、逆德；争者，事之〔末也〕。□□暴□□定仁义也；战国所以立威侵适（敌），弱国之所不能废也。兵者，以武为栋，以文为□；以武为表，以文……以文为内。能审此三者，则知所以胜败矣。武者所以凌（凌）适（敌）分死生也，……危；武者所□□适（敌）也，文者所以守也。兵之用文武也，如乡（响）之应声，而〔影〕之随身也。兵以专壹胜，以离散败。阵以数必固，以疏〔必□〕。将有威则生，失威则死；有威则胜，毋威则败。卒有将则斗，无将则北。……赏罚之胃（谓）也。卒畏将于敌者战胜，卒畏敌于将者战北。未战，所以知胜败，固称将□敌，敌之与将犹权衡也。兵以安静治，以暴疾乱。出卒陈兵，固有恒令；行伍之疏数，固有恒法。先……敌之恒令，非追北衲邑，先后……之恒令，前失后斩。兵之恒陈，有乡（向）适（敌）者，有内乡（向）者，有立陈者，有坐陈〔者〕……将与卒，非有父子之亲、血〔肤〕之树（属），六亲之私也。然而见适（敌），走之如归，前唯（虽）有千仁（仞）之溪，折[月责]脊……〔前则见全明之〕赏，后则见必死之刑。将前不能明其〔令〕，〔后不能见〕其严，则败军死将禽卒也。□□……制，严刑罚，□□赏，全攻发（伐）之得，伸（陈）斧越（钺），饬章旗，有功必〔赏〕，犯令必死。及至两适（敌）之相距，行伸（陈）薄（迫）近，……有天下之善者，不能御大鼓之后矣。出卒伸（陈）兵，行伸（阵）视适（敌），章旗相望，矢弩未合，兵刃未椄（接），先謞者虚，后謞胃（谓）之实，不謞谓之闭。〔闭〕实□□□也。

*Weapons are inauspicious instruments and the negation of virtue; contention is the last of affairs. … establish benevolence and righteousness; the warring states use arms to set up their authority and to invade the enemy, and even a weak state cannot abolish them. In the army, the military is the ridgepole and the civil is the …; the military is the exterior and the civil the interior. One who can examine these three knows the causes of victory and defeat. The military is that by which one overruns the enemy and decides life and death … the military is that by which one attacks the enemy, and the civil is that by which one defends. The army's use of the civil and the military is like an echo answering a sound, and a shadow following the body. The army wins by singleness of purpose and is defeated by dispersal. A formation is made firm by dense ranks and is undone by sparse ones. If the general has authority he lives; if he loses it he dies. With authority he wins; without it he is defeated. If the troops have a general they fight; without one they flee. … this is what reward and punishment mean. When the troops fear the general more than the enemy, they are victorious; when they fear the enemy more than the general, they are routed. Before battle, one can know the outcome of victory and defeat, for the general is weighed against the enemy as on a balance. The army is well ordered when it is calm and quiet, and thrown into disorder by violence and haste. In sending out troops and deploying ranks there are fixed commands; in the density of the ranks there are fixed rules. … the enemy's fixed commands: unless pursuing a routed foe or entering a captured city, … he who falls behind while advancing is beheaded. In the army's fixed formations there are those facing the enemy, those facing inward, those standing, and those seated. … The general and his troops share no bond of father and son, no kinship of blood, no private favor of the six relations. Yet when they see the enemy they rush upon him as if returning home; though before them is a ravine a thousand fathoms deep, they break their backs … if before them they see the full and shining reward, and behind them the punishment of certain death. If the general cannot make his commands clear in front and cannot display his sternness behind, then the army is defeated, the general dies, and the troops are captured. … set strict penalties, … rewards, perfect the gains of attack and assault, display the axes of execution, array the banners and flags; for merit there must be reward, for violating orders there must be death. When the two enemies face each other and the ranks press close, … even one who is skilled in all under heaven cannot hold back the great drum's onslaught. When troops are sent out and ranks deployed, when the formations advance to view the enemy, banners and flags facing each other, before arrows and crossbows are joined and before blades have met—he who shouts first is the empty one, he who shouts later is called the substantial one, and he who does not shout is called the closed one. …*
