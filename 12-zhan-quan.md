## 终极整合版 · Ultimate Reconstructed Text

### 1

**战权:** 兵法曰：「千人而成权，万人而成武。权先加人者，敌不力交；武先加人者，敌无威接。」故兵贵先胜于此，则胜彼矣；弗胜于此，则弗胜彼矣。凡我往则彼来，彼来则我往，相为胜败，此战之理然也。

The art of war says: \"A thousand men form authority; ten thousand men form martial power. When authority is first imposed upon the enemy, they will not have the strength to engage in combat. When martial power is first imposed upon the enemy beforehand, the adversary has no might to resist.\" Therefore, troops are precious in securing victory here before engaging there; thus, they will prevail over the foe. If one cannot secure a victory here, then one cannot achieve victory there either. Whenever we advance, they come; when they come, we advance—mutually determining victories and defeats; this is the natural principle of warfare.

### 2

**战权:** 夫精诚在乎神明，战权在乎道所极。有者无之，无者有之，安所信之。

True sincerity lies in spiritual clarity, and the authority to wage war lies in reaching the ultimate principles of the Dao. Possession becomes non-possession; non-possession becomes possession—where then can one place trust?

### 3

**战权:** 先王之所传闻者，任正去诈，存其慈顺，决无留刑。故知道者，必先图不知止之败，恶在乎必往有功。轻进而求战者，敌复图止，我往而敌制胜矣。故兵法曰：「求而从之，见而加之，主人不敢当而陵之，必丧其权。」

What was passed down by the former kings is to entrust in righteousness and abandon deceit, preserve compassion and obedience, and make decisive judgments without lingering punishments. Therefore, those who understand the Dao must first plan against failures caused by not knowing when to stop; they do not desire success that necessarily comes from advancing recklessly. He who lightly advances and seeks battle will find the enemy prepared to halt him; when I advance, the enemy controls the victory. Therefore, the art of war says: \"If one seeks and follows after them, appears before them and attacks, then even though the enemy is the defender, they will not dare to resist but will be overpowered—thus inevitably losing their authority.\"

### 4

**战权:** 凡夺者无气，恐者不可守；败者无人，兵无道也。意往而不疑则从之，夺敌者无前则加之，明视而高居则威之，兵道极矣。

Generally, those who are deprived of morale have no spirit; those who are fearful cannot hold a defense. The defeated have no people left, for the army has lost its way. When one's intent is resolute and without doubt, follow it; when the enemy has no front to defend, strike them; when clearly observed from a high position, impose authority—this is the ultimate principle of military conduct.

### 5

**战权:** 其言无谨偷矣，其陵犯无节破矣，水溃雷击三军乱矣。必安其危，去其患，以智决之。

Their words are careless and deceitful; their aggression has no restraint, thus easily broken; like water breaching its banks or thunder striking, the three armies fall into chaos. One must stabilize danger and remove threats, deciding matters through wisdom.

### 6

**战权:** 高之以廊庙之论，重之以受命之论，锐之以逾垠之论，则敌国可不战而服。

Elevate it with the discourse of halls and temples, reinforce it with the argument of receiving one's mandate, sharpen it with the reasoning of surpassing boundaries—then enemy states may be subdued without battle.
