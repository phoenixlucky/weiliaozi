# 战威 · Warfare and Authority

## 1

**原文：** 凡兵，有以道胜，有以威胜，有以力胜。讲武料敌，使敌之气失而师散，虽形全而不为之用，此道胜也。审法制，明赏罚，便器用，使民有必战之心，此威胜也。破军杀将，乘闉发机，溃众夺地，成功乃返，此力胜也。王侯如此，所以三胜者毕矣。

**Translation:** In warfare, there are victories achieved through the Way (Dao), victories achieved through authority, and victories achieved through strength. By discussing military matters and assessing the enemy's capabilities, one can cause the enemy to lose morale and disband their forces. Even though the enemy may still have intact formations, they will not be effectively utilized—this is victory through the Way (Dao). Carefully examining military regulations, clarifying rewards and punishments, and ensuring convenient use of equipment instill in the people a determination to fight—this is victory through authority. Destroying enemy armies and killing their generals, seizing fortifications and activating mechanisms, breaking up the enemy's forces and capturing territory, then returning with success—this is victory through strength. When marquises and dukes act in this manner, they have thus achieved all three types of victories.

---

## 2

**原文：** 夫将之所以战者民也，民之所以战者气也。气实则斗，气夺则走。

**Translation:** The reason a general can fight is because of the people; the reason the people can fight is because of their morale. When morale is strong, they will fight; when morale is broken, they will flee.

---

## 3

**原文：** 刑未加，兵未接，而所以夺敌者五：一曰庙胜之论；二曰受命之论；三曰逾垠之论；四曰深沟高垒之论；五曰举阵加刑之论。此五者，先料敌而后动，是以击虚夺之也。

**Translation:** Before punishments are imposed and before weapons clash, there are five ways to demoralize the enemy: first is the argument of victory secured in the temple (i.e., through strategic planning). Second is the argument of receiving a mandate. Third is the argument of crossing boundaries. Fourth is the argument of deep trenches and high fortifications. Fifth is the argument of deploying formations and imposing punishments. These five factors involve first assessing the enemy and then taking action, thus enabling one to strike at weaknesses and demoralize them.

---

## 4

**原文：** 善用兵者，能夺人而不夺于人。夺者心之机也，令者一众心也。众不审则数变，数变则令虽出众不信矣。

**Translation:** A skilled general can demoralize the enemy without being demoralized by them. To demoralize is to seize the initiative of the heart; issuing commands unites the hearts of the people. If the people are not clearly informed, they will often change their minds; if they frequently change their minds, even when orders are issued, the people will no longer believe them.

---

## 5

**原文：** 故令之之法，小过无更，小疑无申。故上无疑令，则众不二听，动无疑事，则众不二志，未有不信其心而能得其力者也，未有不得其力而能致其死战者也。

**Translation:** Therefore, the method of issuing commands is: for minor offenses, do not alter the punishment; for minor doubts, do not reiterate explanations. Therefore, when superiors issue no uncertain orders, the people will not have divided loyalties; when actions involve no doubtful matters, the people will have unified determination. There has never been a case where one did not gain trust in their hearts yet could obtain their strength, nor a case where one failed to obtain their strength yet could lead them to fight to the death.

---

## 6

**原文：** 故国必有礼、信、亲、爱之义，则可以饥易饱；国必有孝、慈、廉、耻之俗，则可以死易生。古者率民必先礼信而后爵禄，先廉耻而后刑罚，先亲爱而后律其身。

**Translation:** Therefore, a state must possess principles of propriety, trustworthiness, kinship, and affection; only then can it transform hunger into satisfaction. A state must have a custom of filial piety, kindness, integrity, and a sense of shame; only then can it transform death into life. In ancient times, leading the people always began with propriety and trustworthiness before bestowing ranks and rewards; it emphasized integrity and a sense of shame before applying punishments; and it stressed affection and love before enforcing self-discipline.

---

## 7

**原文：** 故战者必本乎率身以励众士，如心之使四肢也。志不励则士不死节，士不死节则众不战。

**Translation:** Therefore, in battle one must always begin by setting an example to inspire the soldiers, just as the heart commands the limbs. If determination is not inspired, the soldiers will not die for their principles; if the soldiers do not die for their principles, the masses will not fight.

---

## 8

**原文：** 励士之道，民之生不可不厚也。爵列之等，死丧之亲，民之所营不可不显也。必也因民所生而制之，因民所营而显之，田禄之实，饮食之亲，乡里相劝，死丧相救，兵役相从，此民之所励也。

**Translation:** The way to inspire soldiers is that the people's livelihood must not be neglected. Ranks and distinctions, mourning for relatives who have died—these matters of concern to the people cannot go unrecognized. One must certainly regulate matters according to the people's livelihood and highlight what they value. The reality of land grants and stipends, the intimacy of food and drink, mutual encouragement among neighbors, aiding each other in times of death or mourning, and following one another in military service—these are what motivate the people.

---

## 9

**原文：** 使什伍如亲戚，卒伯如朋友。止如堵墙，动如风雨，车不结辙，士不旋踵，此本战之道也。

**Translation:** Make those in the ten-man and five-man units as close as relatives, and those in the hundred-man unit as close as friends. When stationary, they are solid as a wall; when moving, swift as wind and rain. Wagon tracks do not overlap, and soldiers do not turn back—this is the fundamental principle of warfare.

---

## 10

**原文：** 地所以养民也，城所以守地也，战所以守城也，故务耕者民不饥，务守者地不危，务战者城不围。三者，先王之本务也，本务者兵最急。

**Translation:** Land is used to sustain the people, cities are used to defend land, and battle is used to defend cities. Therefore, by emphasizing farming, the people will not starve; by emphasizing defense, the land will not be endangered; and by emphasizing warfare, the city will not be besieged. These three—farming, defending, and fighting—are the fundamental duties of previous kings; among these duties, military matters are most urgent.

---

## 11

**原文：** 故先王专务于兵，有五焉，委积不多则士不行；赏禄不厚则民不劝；武士不选则众不强；器用不便则力不壮；刑罚不中则众不畏。务此五者，静能守其所固，动能成其所欲。

**Translation:** Therefore, previous kings focused especially on military affairs, and there were five aspects to this: if stored supplies are not abundant, the soldiers cannot march. If rewards and stipends are not generous, the people will lack encouragement. If warriors are not carefully selected, the forces will not be strong. If weapons and equipment are inconvenient to use, strength cannot be maximized. If punishments are not properly applied, the people will not fear them. Attending to these five matters ensures that when at rest, one can defend what is already secured; and when acting, one can achieve desired objectives.

---

## 12

**原文：** 夫以居攻出，则居欲重，阵欲坚，发欲毕，斗欲齐。

**Translation:** When defending from a position to attack the enemy outside, then defense must be solid, formations must be firm, attacks must be decisive, and combat must be coordinated.

---

## 13

**原文：** 王国富民，伯国富士，谨存之国富大夫，亡国富食府，所谓上满下漏，患无所救。

**Translation:** A king's state enriches its people; a hegemon's state enriches its soldiers. A cautious and enduring state enriches its officials, while a doomed state enriches only the granaries—this is what is called "the upper being full while the lower leaks," a condition from which there is no remedy for disaster.

---

## 14

**原文：** 故曰："举贤任能，不时日而事利；明法审令，不卜筮而事吉；贵功养劳，不祷祠而得福。"又曰："天时不如地利，地利不如人和。"圣人所贵，人事而已。

**Translation:** Therefore it is said: "Promoting virtuous and capable individuals ensures success without needing to consult auspicious days; Clarifying laws and issuing clear commands brings good fortune without the need for divination; Valuing achievements and rewarding labor brings blessings without the need to pray or make sacrifices." It is also said: "Favorable weather conditions are not as important as geographical advantages, and geographical advantages are not as crucial as harmony among people." What sages value most is human effort alone.

---

## 15

**原文：** 夫勤劳之师，将必先己，暑不张盖，寒不重衣，险必下步，军井成而后饮，军食熟而后饭，军垒成而后舍，劳佚必以身同之。如此，则师虽久，而不老不弊。

**Translation:** A hardworking army will have a general who leads by example. In summer, he does not use an umbrella; in winter, he does not wear extra clothing. On dangerous terrain, he dismounts and walks. He drinks only after the soldiers' wells are dug, eats only after their food is cooked, and takes shelter only after the fortifications are completed. His labor and rest must always be shared with his troops. If a general acts in this way, then even though the campaign lasts long, the army will not grow weary or decline.

## 📖 群书治要异文

《群书治要》卷三十七收录的《战威》篇与武经七书本内容编排不同。群书治要本选录了关于"命令"和"动事"的两个段落：

### 1

**战威:** 令所以一衆心也。不审所出，则数变，数变则令虽出，衆不信也。出令之法，虽有小过毋更，小疑毋申，事所以待衆力也。不审所动则数变，数变，则事虽起，衆不安也。动事之法，虽有小过毋更，小难毋戚，故上无疑令，则衆不二听，动无疑事，则衆不二志。

*The reason for issuing orders is to unify the people's hearts. If the source is not clear, then the orders will frequently change; if they frequently change, then even though orders are issued, the people will not believe them. The method of issuing orders is such that even if there is a minor mistake, it should not be changed; even if there is slight doubt, it should not be reiterated. This is how matters are handled in order to await the collective effort of the people. If one does not carefully consider what actions to take, then plans will frequently change; if there are frequent changes, then even though matters begin, the people will remain unsettled. The method for undertaking actions is such that even if there are minor mistakes, they should not be altered; even if there are small difficulties, one should not become distressed. Therefore, when the superior issues no uncertain orders, the people will have no divided loyalties; and when actions taken involve no uncertainty, the people's resolve will remain undivided.*
