# 兵教下 · Military Training, Part II

> **终极整合版**：以武经七书本为底本。本章仅存武经七书本一种版本，无其他版本异文，整合版即其全文。
>
> *Ultimate reconstructed text: based on the Wujing Qishu recension. This chapter survives only in the Wujing Qishu recension, with no variant readings from other witnesses, so the reconstructed text is simply its complete text.*

## 终极整合版 · Ultimate Reconstructed Text

### 1

**兵教下:** 臣闻人君有必胜之道，故能并兼广大，以一其制度，则威加天下有十二焉：

I have heard that a sovereign who possesses an invincible way can thus unify and expand widely, unifying his system of regulations; then there are twelve ways by which he extends his authority over the world.

### 2

**兵教下:** 一曰连刑，谓同罪保伍也；二曰地禁，谓禁止行道，以网外奸也；三曰全军，谓甲首相附，三五相同，以结其联也；四曰开塞，谓分地以限，各死其职而坚守也；五曰分限，谓左右相禁，前后相待，垣车为固，以逆以止也；六曰号别，谓前列务进以别，其后者不得争先登不次也；七曰五章，谓彰明行列，始卒不乱也；八曰全曲，谓曲折相从，皆有分部也；九曰金鼓，谓兴有功，致有德也；十曰陈车，谓接连前矛，马冒其目也；十一曰死士，谓众军之中有材智者，乘于战车，前后纵横，出奇制敌也；十二曰力卒，谓经旗全曲，不麾不动也。

First is \"lianxing,\" meaning mutual legal responsibility among five households, where those who commit the same crime guarantee each other's accountability. Second is \"dijin,\" meaning restrictions on movement along roads to prevent external misconduct. Third is \"quan jun,\" meaning armor and helmets are closely attached, with three or five units unified in formation to strengthen their cohesion. Fourth is \"kaise,\" meaning dividing the land into sectors as boundaries, where each unit dies for its post and firmly holds its position. Fifth is \"fenxian,\" meaning the left and right sides restrain each other, the front and rear support one another, with walled chariots forming a solid defense to resist and halt the enemy. Sixth is \"haobie,\" meaning those in the front ranks must advance clearly to distinguish themselves, and those behind may not recklessly compete for precedence or disrupt order. Seventh is \"wuzhang,\" meaning the ranks and formations are clearly marked so that from beginning to end, there is no disorder. Eighth is \"quanqu,\" meaning units follow each other in winding paths and all have designated divisions. Ninth is \"jingu,\" meaning the use of bells and drums to rally those with merit and honor those with virtue. Tenth is \"chenche,\" meaning chariots are connected in front of the spear formations, with horses advancing so fiercely that they obscure their own vision. Eleventh is \"shishi,\" meaning among the troops, those with talent and wisdom ride in war chariots, moving freely forward and backward, left and right, to launch unexpected attacks that subdue the enemy. Twelfth is \"liche,\" meaning soldiers under the standard of a complete formation remain motionless unless given orders to move.

### 3

**兵教下:** 此十二者教成，犯令不舍。兵弱能强之，主卑能尊之，令弊能起之，民流能亲之，人众能治之，地大能守之。国车不出于阃，组甲不出于橐，而威服天下矣。

Once these twelve principles are fully trained, no one who violates commands will be spared. A weak army can become strong, a humble ruler can gain respect, flawed commands can be reformed, wandering people can be brought together, a large population can be governed, and an extensive territory can be defended. The state's chariots need not leave the gate, nor armor be taken from its case, and yet the authority will subdue the world.

### 4

**兵教下:** 兵有五致：为将忘家，逾垠忘亲，指敌忘身，必死则生，急胜为下。

There are five principles of warfare: a general who forgets his family, one who crosses boundaries forgetting kinship, one who faces the enemy forgetting self-preservation, for certain death brings life, and urgent victory is achieved by subordinates.

### 5

**兵教下:** 百人被刃，陷行乱陈，千人被刃，擒敌杀将，万人被刃，横行天下。

A hundred men wielding weapons can break through enemy ranks and create chaos; a thousand armed men can capture the enemy and kill their general; ten thousand armed men can roam unchallenged across the world.

### 6

**兵教下:** 武王问太公望曰：'吾欲少间而极用人之要？'望对曰：'赏如山，罚如溪。太上无过，其次补过，使人无得私语。诸罚而请不罚者死，诸赏而请不赏者死。伐国必因其变，示之以财，以观其穷，示之以弊，以观其病，上乖下离，若此之类是伐之因也。'

King Wu asked Tai Gong Wang, \"I wish to briefly understand the essentials of using people.\" Wang replied, \"Rewards should be as solid as a mountain, and punishments as swift as a stream. The highest level is to have no faults; the next best is to correct them, ensuring that people cannot engage in private conversations. Those who commit punishable offenses and request exemption from punishment shall be put to death; those who deserve rewards but request not to receive them shall also be put to death. To attack a state, one must take advantage of its changes. Show it wealth to observe its desperation; show it weaknesses to see its vulnerabilities. When the ruler is discordant and the people are divided, such conditions are the causes for launching an attack.\"

### 7

**兵教下:** 凡兴师，必审内外之权，以计其去。兵有备阙，粮食有馀不足，校所出入之路，然后兴师伐乱，必能入之。

Whenever a military campaign is launched, one must carefully examine the internal and external circumstances to calculate when it should be abandoned. When troops are prepared or have deficiencies, and grain supplies are in surplus or shortage, the routes of entry and exit must be assessed. Only then can one launch a campaign against disorder and surely succeed in entering the enemy's territory.

### 8

**兵教下:** 地大而城小者，必先收其地。城大而窄者，必先攻其城。地广而人寡者，则绝其厄。地狭而人众者，则筑大堙以临之。无丧其利，无奋其时，宽其政，夷其业；救其弊，则足施天下。

When the land is vast but the city small, one must first secure its territory. When the city is large but narrow, one must first attack its walls. When the land is extensive but the population sparse, one should cut off its strategic passes. When the land is narrow and the people numerous, then build a large earthwork to overlook them. Do not lose your advantage; do not act recklessly at inopportune times. Ease the administration and level their industries; correct their weaknesses, and this will be sufficient to extend influence throughout the world.

### 9

**兵教下:** 今战国相攻，大伐有德。自伍而两，自两而师，不一其令。率俾民心不定，徒尚骄侈，谋患辨讼，吏究其事，累且败也。日暮路远，还有挫气。师老将贪，争掠易败。

Nowadays, warring states attack each other, and great conquests target those with virtue. From five men to ten, from ten to a company, commands are not unified. This leads the people's hearts to be unstable; they merely indulge in pride and extravagance, plotting against each other and engaging in disputes. Officials investigate these matters, resulting only in repeated failures. As dusk falls and the road is long, there will also be a loss of morale. When troops grow weary and generals become greedy, they easily quarrel over spoils and thus face defeat.

### 10

**兵教下:** 凡将轻，垒卑，众动，可攻也。将重，垒高，众惧，可围也。凡围必开其小利，使渐夷弱，则节各有不食者矣。众夜击者惊也，众避事者离也。待人之救，期战而蹙，皆心失而伤气也。伤气败军，曲谋败国。

Whenever a general is reckless, the fortifications are low, and the troops are restless, an attack is feasible. When a general is cautious, the defenses are strong, and the soldiers are fearful, then they can be encircled. Whenever an enemy is surrounded, one must offer small advantages to entice them gradually into weakness; then each section will have those who go hungry. When many soldiers attack at night, it is a sign of panic; when many avoid their duties, it indicates disunity. Waiting for others' rescue and expecting battle yet being pressed—these are all signs of lost morale and wounded spirit. Loss of morale leads to the defeat of an army; flawed strategies lead to the downfall of a state.
