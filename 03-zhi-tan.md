# 制谈 · Zhidan (On Institutions)

## 1

**制谈:** 凡兵，制必先定，制先定则士不乱，士不乱则形乃明。金鼓所指，则百人尽斗。陷行乱阵，则千人尽斗。覆军杀将，则万人齐刃。天下莫能当其战矣。

In all military affairs, the system must first be established; when the system is firmly in place, the soldiers will not become disorderly. When the soldiers are not disorderly, then the situation becomes clear. Where the gongs and drums point, a hundred men will all fight. When lines are broken and formations become chaotic, then a thousand men will all engage in battle. When an army is crushed and its general killed, ten thousand men will all raise their weapons together. No one under heaven can withstand the force of such a battle anymore.

## 2

**制谈:** 古者，士有什伍，车有偏列，鼓鸣旗麾，先登者未尝非多力国士也，先死者亦未尝非多力国士也。

In ancient times, soldiers were organized in groups of ten and five; chariots were arranged in formations. When the drums sounded and the banners waved, those who led the charge had never failed to be brave men from a powerful state, nor had those who fell first ever failed to be such brave men.

## 3

**制谈:** 损敌一人，而损我百人，此资敌而伤甚焉，世将不能禁。征役分军而逃归，或临战自北，则逃伤甚焉，世将不能禁。杀人于百步之外者弓矢也，杀人于五十步之内者矛戟也，将已鼓而士卒相嚣，拗矢折矛抱戟，利后发，战，有此数者，内自败也，世将不能禁。士失什伍，车失偏列，奇兵捐将而走，大众亦走，世将不能禁。夫将能禁此四者，则高山陵之，深水绝之，坚阵犯之。不能禁此四者，犹亡舟楫，绝江河，不可得也。

If one enemy is defeated at the cost of a hundred of our own, this is aiding the enemy while suffering great losses—such a situation generals of the world have been unable to prevent. When soldiers on campaign split off and flee back, or when they turn tail in the face of battle, this results in great losses and injuries—such behavior generals of the world have also been unable to prevent. Those who kill from a hundred paces away do so with bows and arrows; those who kill within fifty paces use spears and halberds. When the general has already sounded the drum but the soldiers shout in confusion, when they break their bowstrings, snap their spear shafts, clutch their halberds, and favor striking from a disadvantageous position—when battle is fought with these several factors present, it is an internal collapse; such defeat generals of the world have not been able to prevent. When soldiers lose their group formations, when chariots abandon their proper arrangements, and when irregular troops desert the general and flee, the main force will also run—such a collapse generals of the world have not been capable of preventing. A general who can prevent these four situations will be able to scale high mountains, cross deep waters, and confront strong formations. One who cannot prevent these four things is like a man without boats or oars attempting to cross rivers—such success is impossible to achieve.

## 4

**制谈:** 民非乐死而恶生也，号令明，法制审，故能使之前。明赏于前，决罚于后，是以发能中利，动则有功。

The people do not enjoy death and despise life; it is only through clear orders and well-established laws that they can be led to advance. When rewards are clearly announced beforehand and punishments firmly carried out afterward, therefore, when troops are dispatched they hit the mark of advantage, and whenever they act, there is achievement.

## 5

**制谈:** 令百人一卒，千人一司马，万人一将，以少诛众，以弱诛强。试听臣言，其术足使三军之众，诛一人无失刑，父不敢舍子，子不敢舍父，况国人乎？

Issue orders so that one officer commands a hundred men, one military commissioner leads a thousand, and one general commands ten thousand. Thus, the few can punish the many, and the weak can subdue the strong. Hear my words and test them; the method is sufficient to ensure that among three armies, when punishing a single person no injustice will be done. A father dares not abandon his son, nor does a son dare to leave his father—how much more so for the people of the state?

## 6

**制谈:** 一贼仗剑击于市，万人无不避之者，臣谓：‘非一人之独勇，万人皆不肖也’。何则？‘必死与必生，固不侔也。’听臣之术，足使三军之众为一死贼，莫当其前，莫随其后，而能独出独入焉。独出独入者，王伯之兵也。

If one bandit brandishes a sword and attacks in the marketplace, ten thousand people will all avoid him. I say: "It is not that this single man is uniquely brave, but rather that the ten thousand are all weak." Why so? "Those who must die and those who must live are naturally not equal." Hear my method, and it will be sufficient to make the entire army act as a single determined bandit—none shall dare stand before them, none shall follow behind, and they will be able to move freely in and out at will. To move freely in and out alone is the army of a king or hegemon.

## 7

**制谈:** 有提九万之众，而天下莫能当者，谁？曰：‘桓公也。’有提七万之众，而天下莫敢当者，谁？曰：‘吴起也。’有提三万之众，而天下莫敢当者，谁？曰：‘武子也。’今天下诸国士所率无不及二十万众者，然不能济功名者，不明乎禁舍开塞也。明其制，一人胜之，则十人亦以胜之也。十人胜之，则百千万人亦以胜之也。故曰：‘便吾器用，养吾武勇，发之如鸟击，如赴千仞之溪。’

Who is there who commands an army of ninety thousand, yet no one under heaven can resist? It is said: "Duke Huan." Who is there who commands an army of seventy thousand, yet none under heaven dares to oppose? It is said: "Wu Qi." Who is there who commands an army of thirty thousand, yet none under heaven dare to resist? It is said: "Wu Zi." Now, the soldiers of all states under heaven lead no fewer than two hundred thousand men; yet none can achieve success or fame because they do not understand how to control halting and advancing, opening and closing. Clarify the system: if one man can overcome an enemy, then ten men can also overcome him. If ten men can overcome him, then a hundred, a thousand, or even ten thousand men can also overcome him. Therefore it is said: "Facilitate our weapons and equipment, nurture our martial bravery; launch them like a bird striking, as if rushing toward a deep ravine a thousand feet below."

## 8

**制谈:** 今国被患者，以重币出聘，以爱子出质，以地界出割，得天下助，卒名为十万，其实不过数万尔。其兵来者，无不谓将者曰：‘无为人下，先战。’其实不可得而战也。

Now, when a state is suffering from trouble, it sends out generous gifts to seek alliances, offers beloved sons as hostages, and cedes territory. Though it gains help from all under heaven and proclaims its army to number one hundred thousand, in reality the force does not exceed several tens of thousands. When their troops arrive, none fails to say to the generals: "Do not take a lower position; fight first." In reality, however, they cannot engage in battle.

## 9

**制谈:** 量吾境内之民，无伍莫能正矣。经制十万之众，而王必能使之衣吾衣，食吾食。战不胜，守不固者，非吾民之罪，内自致也。天下诸国助我战，犹良骥騄耳之駃，彼驽马鬐兴角逐，何能绍吾后哉？

Assessing the people within our borders, without proper organization they can never be made orderly. With a properly organized force of one hundred thousand men, the king will certainly be able to have them wear our clothing and eat our food. If in battle we are defeated or in defense we fail, it is not the fault of our people; rather, it arises from within ourselves. The various states under heaven aiding us in battle are like the swift steeds Lingji and Luer, while those slow horses with erect manes that attempt to race alongside them—how could they possibly follow our lead?

## 10

**制谈:** 吾用天下之用为用，吾制天下之制为制，修吾号令，明吾刑赏，使天下非农所得食，非战无所得爵，使民扬臂争出农、战，而天下无敌矣。故曰：‘发号出令，信行国内。’

I use the resources of all under heaven as my own, and establish systems that govern all under heaven. I refine my orders, clarify rewards and punishments, ensuring that none under heaven may eat without farming or receive rank without fighting. Thus, I will inspire the people to raise their arms and eagerly leave agriculture for battle, making our state invincible in the world. Therefore it is said: "Issue orders and proclamations that are faithfully carried out within the country."

## 11

**制谈:** 民言有可以胜敌者，毋许其空言，必试其能战也。

When the people speak of being able to defeat the enemy, do not accept empty words; it is essential to test their ability to fight.

## 12

**制谈:** 视人之地而有之，分人之民而畜之，必能内有其贤者也。不能内有其贤，而欲有天下，必覆军杀将。如此，虽战胜而国益弱，得地而国益贫，由国中之制弊矣。

To take possession of another's land and raise as one's own the people of others—this can only be achieved if there are truly capable individuals within. Without having capable men internally, yet desiring to possess all under heaven, one will inevitably suffer the destruction of an army and the death of a general. In such cases, even if victories are won, the state becomes weaker; even if land is gained, the country grows poorer—this arises from flawed internal systems.

## 📖 类书引文

宋代《太平御览》引《尉缭子》佚文两条，对应本篇第3段"杀人于百步之外者弓矢也，杀人于五十步之内者矛戟也"：

**太平御览·兵部八十一·箭下引《尉缭子》曰：** 夫杀人百步之外者，谁也？曰："矢也。"

*The Taiping Yulan (Arrows) quotes the Wei Liaozi: He who kills a man from a hundred paces away—who is it? The answer: "The arrow."*

**太平御览·兵部八十四·戟下引《尉缭子》曰：** 夫杀之五十步之内者，谁也？曰："矛戟也。"

*The Taiping Yulan (Halberds) quotes the Wei Liaozi: He who kills a man from within fifty paces—who is it? The answer: "The spear and halberd."*

> 异文：太平御览以问答体出之，较武经七书本更近于口语，或为古本原貌。
