# 兵令下 · Military Orders, Part II

## 1

**兵令下:** 诸去大军为前御之备者，边县列候各相去三、五里。闻大军为前御之备战，则皆禁行，所以安内也。

The troops guarding the front were deployed in such a way that the outposts along the border counties were spaced three to five li apart. When hearing of preparations for battle by the main force guarding the front, all travel was prohibited; this was done to ensure internal stability.

## 2

**兵令下:** 内卒出戍，令将吏授旗鼓戈甲。发日，后将吏及出县封界者，以坐后戍法。兵戍边一岁，遂亡不候代者，法比亡军。父母妻子知之，与同罪。弗知，赦之。

When conscripts were dispatched for garrison duty, orders were given that officers and officials should present them with banners, drums, halberds, and armor. On the day of departure, if an officer or official failed to accompany the conscripts beyond the county's boundary as required, they would be punished according to the law for delaying garrison duty. A soldier on border garrison duty who remained away without waiting for a replacement after one year was punished according to the same law as deserting from the army. If parents, spouses, or children knew about it and did not report it, they would share the same punishment. Those who were unaware of it would be pardoned.

## 3

**兵令下:** 卒后将吏而至大将所一日，父母妻子尽同罪。卒逃归至家一日，父母妻子弗捕执及不言，亦同罪。

If a soldier arrived at the headquarters of the general one day after his officer or official, then all his parents, spouse, and children would be held equally guilty. If a soldier fled back home for one day and his parents, spouse, or children neither captured him nor reported it, they would also be held equally guilty.

## 4

**兵令下:** 诸战而亡其将吏者，及将吏弃卒独北者，尽斩之。前吏弃其卒而北，后吏能斩之而夺其卒者，赏。军无功者，戍三岁。

All soldiers who fled from battle and left their officers or officials behind, as well as officers or officials who abandoned their troops and retreated alone, were to be executed. If a later officer killed an earlier officer who had fled from battle and abandoned his troops, and took command of those troops, he would be rewarded. Troops that achieved no merit in battle were to serve on garrison duty for three years.

## 5

**兵令下:** 三军大战，若大将死，而从吏五百人以上不能死敌者，斩。大将左右近卒在陈中者，皆斩。馀士卒，有军功者，夺一级。无军功者，戍三岁。

In the case of a major battle involving all three armies, if the supreme general died and more than 500 subordinate officers failed to fight to their death against the enemy, they were to be executed. All soldiers stationed near the supreme general on the battlefield were to be executed if they survived. For other soldiers, those who had previously received military merit would have one level of their rank revoked. Those without any record of military merit were to serve on garrison duty for a period of three years.

## 6

**兵令下:** 战亡伍人，及伍人战死不得其死，同伍尽夺其功。得其尸，罪皆赦。

If five soldiers in a unit fled from battle, or if all five were killed and their deaths could not be verified, then the entire unit would lose all its recorded merits. If their bodies were recovered, all those involved would be pardoned of their crimes.

## 7

**兵令下:** 军之利害，在国之名实。今名在官，而实在家，官不得其实，家不得其名。聚卒为军，有空名而无实，外不足以御敌，内不足以守国，此军之所以不给，将之所以夺威也。

The gains and losses in military affairs depend on the distinction between a state's reputation and its actual capabilities. Now, names are recorded as being in official positions while their actual persons remain at home; the officials do not have their real presence, and those at home do not possess the title. When troops are gathered to form an army, if there are only nominal names without real personnel, such a force is insufficient externally to repel the enemy and internally to defend the state. This is why armies fail to be adequately supplied and why generals lose their authority.

## 8

**兵令下:** 臣以谓卒逃归者，同舍伍人及吏，罚入粮为饶。名为军实，是有一军之名，而有二实之出，国内空虚，自竭民岁，曷以免奔北之祸乎？

I believe that when a soldier flees back home, his fellow soldiers in the same unit and the officers should be punished by contributing additional provisions as compensation. Calling it a military force in name means having the title of one army while actually bearing the burden of two. This leaves the state internally depleted, drains the people's annual resources year after year—how can such a situation avoid the calamity of defeat and rout?

## 9

**兵令下:** 今以法止逃归，禁亡军，是兵之一胜也。什伍相联，及战斗则卒吏相救，是兵之二胜也。将能立威，卒能节制，号令明信，攻守皆得，是兵之三胜也。

Now, by using laws to prevent desertion and prohibit soldiers from fleeing the army, this constitutes one advantage in military strength. When soldiers are organized into groups of ten and five with mutual responsibility, and during battle officers and troops support each other, this constitutes a second advantage in military strength. When generals can establish authority, soldiers can be properly controlled, commands are clear and trustworthy, and both attack and defense are effectively executed, this constitutes a third advantage in military strength.

## 10

**兵令下:** 臣闻古之善用兵者，能杀士卒之半，其次杀其十三，其下杀其十一。能杀其半者，威加海内；杀十三者，力加诸侯；杀十一者，令行士卒。故曰：百万之众不用命，不如万人之斗也。万人之斗，不如百人之奋也。

I have heard that ancient skillful generals in the use of troops could sacrifice half their soldiers, next best a third, and at the very least a tenth. Those who could sacrifice half their forces would extend their authority throughout the realm; those who sacrificed a third of their troops would exert power over the feudal lords; and those who sacrificed a tenth could ensure that commands were obeyed by the soldiers. Therefore it is said: an army of one million unwilling to follow orders is not as effective as ten thousand determined fighters. An army of ten thousand determined fighters is not as effective as a hundred who fight with utmost resolve.

## 11

**兵令下:** "赏如日月，信如四时，令如斧钺，制如干将，士卒不用命者，未之闻也。"

"Rewards should be as clear as the sun and moon, trustworthiness as constant as the four seasons, commands as sharp as axes and halberds, discipline as precise as Ganjiang's craftsmanship. As for soldiers who do not obey orders—such cases have never been heard of."

*… all counties within a hundred li of the army must make provision for defense, like a city living on the frontier. … When an order is given to raise the army, the generals and officers receive the drums and banners … afterward their generals and officers pass beyond the borders of the county … if a man deserts and flees without following his general and officers, he is judged the same as one who abandons the army. His parents … afterward when the generals and officers reach the commander-in-chief's post within one day, … the officers serve a year at the frontier. If in battle one loses his general and officers, or the general and officers die in battle and the troops alone flee and return, the law is that they shall all be beheaded. If a general or officer leads his troops in flight, the general is beheaded … three years. In a great battle, if the commander-in-chief dies, all the five hundred and more who did not die facing the enemy shall be beheaded; and the close attendants of the commander-in-chief … shall all be beheaded. … one merit is stripped away; those without … three years. … those who win military merit serve three years; if one recovers his corpse, the crime is pardoned. When troops flee and return home … this is a wound to the army and a great expense to the state; and if the general cannot forbid it, this is the way of weakening oneself from within. One's name is in the army while in fact one lives at home; … one cannot get the truth of the matter. … the officers take their grain as surplus while in fact they eat at home. In name there is the support of a single army, but in fact there are two sets of expenditures; within the state the granaries are emptied and drained, and the yearly accounting of losses turns into defeat from within. To be able to stop desertion and forbid abandonment of the army is one victory of the army. Make the squads of five and ten mutually linked, make clear … commands strict and trustworthy, rewards for merit … one who can kill a little less than half (of his own troops) extends his power over the feudal lords; one who can kill a tenth … I have heard: a million troops that will not fight are not as good as the corpses of ten thousand; ten thousand that will not die are not as good as the ghosts of a hundred. … trust comparable to the four seasons, commands as stern as axes and halberds, sharp as the swords Gan and Jiang; that troops would not die in such service has never been heard of …*

## 📖 群书治要异文

《群书治要》卷三十七收录的《兵令》篇与武经七书本内容差异较大。群书治要本《兵令》实为武经七书本《兵令上》与《兵令下》的精要选录，共3段：

### 1

**兵令:** 兵者、凶器也。战者、逆德也。争者、事之末也。王者所以伐暴乱而定仁义也。战国所以立威侵敌也。弱国所以不能废。

*War is a weapon of violence. Battle is contrary to virtue. Strife is the last resort of affairs. A true ruler wages war to quell violence and disorder, thereby establishing benevolence and righteousness. Warring states wage wars to establish authority and encroach upon their enemies. Weak states are therefore unable to be eliminated.*

### 2

**兵令:** 兵者，以武为植，以文为种，以武为表，以文为里，以武为外，以文为内，能审此二者，知所以胜败矣。武者所以凌敌分死生也。文者所以视利害，观安危，武者所以犯敌也。文者所以守之也。兵用文武也。如响之应声也。如影之随身也。

*Warfare relies on military strength as its foundation and civil administration as its root; it uses the military as an outward display and civil governance as its inner core. He who can discern these two aspects understands the reasons for victory or defeat. Militarism is used to overpower the enemy and determine life and death. Civil governance is used to assess benefits and harms, observe safety and danger; militarism is employed to confront the enemy. Civil governance is what is used to defend and preserve the state. Warfare employs both civil administration and military strength. It is like an echo responding to a sound. It is like a shadow following the body.*

### 3

**兵令:** 将有威则生，无威则死，有威则胜，无威则败，卒有将则斗，无将则北，有将则死，无将则辱，威者赏罚之谓也。卒畏将（将下旧无甚字，补之）甚于敌者战胜，卒畏敌甚于将者战北，夫战而知所以胜败者，固称将于敌也。敌之与将也。犹权衡也。将之于卒也。非有父母之恻，血肤之属，六亲之私，然而见敌走之如归，前虽有千仞之溪，不测之渊，见入汤火如蹈者，前见全明之赏，后见必死之刑也。将之能制士卒，其在军营之内，行阵之间，明庆赏，严刑罚，陈斧钺，饰章旗，有功必赏，犯令必死，及至两敌相至，行阵薄近，将提枹而鼓之，存亡生死，存枹之端矣。虽有天下善兵者，不能图大鼓之后矣。

*A general with authority brings life; without authority, death. With authority, one wins; without it, defeat. Soldiers under a capable commander will fight; without such leadership, they will flee in rout. Under a good leader, soldiers may die for their cause; without one, they face disgrace. Authority refers to the implementation of rewards and punishments. Soldiers who fear their commander (the original text lacks the character "shen" after "wei jiang," which has been added) more than they fear the enemy will achieve victory; soldiers who fear the enemy more than their commander will be routed in battle. In warfare, one who understands the reasons for victory and defeat must certainly regard his general as superior to the enemy. The enemy and the general are like a balance scale. The general and his soldiers are not bound by the compassion of parent to child, nor by blood relations or familial ties. Yet when they see the enemy, they flee as if returning home; even before them lies a ravine thousands of feet deep or an abyss of unknown depth, yet they would enter boiling water and fire as though treading on solid ground—because ahead awaits certain rewards for those who remain whole and bright, while behind looms inevitable punishment by death. A general's ability to control his soldiers lies in the camp and battlefield: he must clearly announce rewards, strictly enforce punishments, display axes and halberds, decorate banners and standards. Those who achieve merit will surely be rewarded; those who violate orders will face certain death. When two opposing forces meet on the field, their lines drawing close, the general raises his drumstick to beat the war drum—then life or death, survival or destruction, all depend upon this single stroke of the drum. Even the most skilled military strategist in the world cannot plan for what happens after the great drum has been struck.*

> 主要异文：武经七书本将《兵令》分为上下两篇共18段，群书治要本合为一篇3段。群书治要本第1段对应武经七书本《兵令上》第1段，但有"争者、事之末也""弱国所以不能废"等独有文句；第2段"以武为植，以文为种"对应《兵令上》第2段；第3段融合了《兵令上》第3-6段及《兵令下》关于赏罚的思想。

## 📖 类书引文

宋代《太平御览·兵部二十七·法令》引《尉缭子》佚文，对应本篇第10-11段，但文字有重要差异，可资校勘：

**太平御览引《尉缭子》曰：** 古之善用兵者，能杀卒之半，其次杀其十三，其下杀其十一。杀其半者威立海内，杀其十三者力加诸侯，杀其十一者令行士卒。故曰百万之众不斗，不如万人之尸；万人之死，不如百人之贼。赏明如日月，信比如四时，令严如斧钺，而出卒有不死者，未尝闻也。

*The Taiping Yulan quotes the Wei Liaozi: Ancients who were skilled in using troops could sacrifice half their soldiers, next best a third, and at the very least a tenth. Those who sacrificed half established their authority throughout the realm; those who sacrificed a third exerted power over the feudal lords; those who sacrificed a tenth ensured commands were obeyed by the soldiers. Therefore it is said: an army of a million that does not fight is not as good as ten thousand corpses; ten thousand dead men are not as good as a hundred bandits. Rewards should be as bright as the sun and moon, trustworthiness as constant as the four seasons, orders as strict as axes and halberds—yet there have been soldiers who did not die in battle—such a thing has never been heard of.*

> 异文分析：「不如万人之尸」「不如百人之贼」较武经七书本「不如万人之斗」「不如百人之奋」更为峻烈，与尉缭子重刑思想一致；「赏明如日月，信比如四时，令严如斧钺」与武经七书本「赏如日月，信如四时，令如斧钺」用字略异而意同；末句「而出卒有不死者，未尝闻也」则与「士卒不用命者，未之闻也」互为表里。
