# 武议 · Martial Wisdom

## 1

**武议:** 凡兵不攻无过之城，不杀无罪之人。夫杀人之父兄，利人之财货，臣妾人之子女，此皆盗也。故兵者所以诛乱禁不义也。兵之所加者，农不离其田业，贾不离其肆宅，士大夫不离其官府，由其武议在于一人，故兵不血刃，而天下亲焉。

All armies do not attack cities without fault, and do not kill people without guilt. Killing the fathers and elder brothers of others, taking advantage of their wealth, and enslaving their sons and daughters—all these are acts of banditry. Therefore, armies exist to punish disorder and prohibit injustice. Where the army is deployed, farmers do not leave their fields and occupations, merchants do not abandon their shops or homes, and scholars and officials do not depart from their government offices. This is because martial authority rests in one person; thus, weapons are never drawn, yet all under heaven remain loyal.

## 2

**武议:** 万乘农战，千乘救守，百乘事养。农战不外索权，救守不外索助，事养不外索资。

A ten-thousand-chariot state engages in agricultural warfare, a thousand-chariot state focuses on rescue and defense, and a hundred-chariot state is occupied with service and sustenance. Agricultural warfare does not seek power from outside, rescue and defense do not seek assistance from outside, and service and sustenance do not seek resources from outside.

## 3

**武议:** 夫出不足战，入不足守者，治之以市。市者，所以给战守也。万乘无千乘之助，必有百乘之市。

Those who are insufficient for external warfare and internal defense should be managed through the market. The market exists to provide for war and defense. A ten-thousand-chariot state, without the aid of a thousand-chariot force, must have a hundred-chariot market.

## 4

**武议:** 凡诛者所以明武也，杀一人而三军震者，杀之。杀一人而万人喜者，杀之。杀之贵大，赏之贵小，当杀而虽贵重必杀之，是刑上究也。赏及牛童马圉者，是赏下流也。夫能刑上究赏下流，此将之武也，故人主重将。

Punishment is used to demonstrate martial authority; if killing one person causes the entire army to be awed, then kill him. If killing one person brings joy to ten thousand people, then kill him. Execution should target the prominent; rewards should benefit the humble. If someone deserves execution, even if they are noble and influential, they must be killed—this is the principle of impartial punishment. Rewarding even cowherds and stable boys is rewarding those at the lowest level. A general who can enforce punishment upon the high-ranking and bestow rewards on the lowly demonstrates martial virtue; therefore, sovereigns value their generals highly.

## 5

**武议:** 夫将提鼓挥枹，临难决战，接兵角刃，鼓之而当，则赏功立名，鼓之而不当，则身死国亡。是存亡安危在于枹端，奈何无重将也。

A general who raises the drum and wields the baton, faces danger in decisive battle, confronts the enemy with weapons drawn—if he commands effectively, then merit is achieved and a name is made; if his command is ineffective, then death follows and the state perishes. Thus, survival or destruction, safety or peril, rest on the tip of the baton—how can one not value generals highly?

## 6

**武议:** 夫提鼓挥枹，接兵角刃，居以武事成功者，臣以为非难也。古人曰：'无蒙冲而攻，无渠答而守。是谓无善之军。视无见，听无闻，由国无市也。夫市也者，百货之官也，市贱卖贵，以限士人。人食粟一斗，马食菽三斗，人有饥色，马有瘠形，何也？市有所出，而官无主也。夫提天下之节制，而无百货之官，无谓其能战也。

To raise the drum and wield the baton, to confront the enemy in battle, and to achieve success through martial endeavors—this, I believe, is not difficult. Ancients said: \"To attack without ramming vessels is impossible; to defend without palisades and barriers is also impossible.\" This is called an army lacking in competence. To see nothing, to hear nothing—this is like a state without a market. The market is the official domain of all goods; buying cheap and selling expensive limits the people. A person consumes one dou of millet, while a horse consumes three dou of beans; yet people show signs of hunger and horses appear emaciated—why is this? The market has goods for sale, but the officials lack oversight. To hold authority over the entire realm's logistics yet have no official in charge of all goods—this cannot be called a capacity for war.

## 7

**武议:** 起兵，直使甲胄生虮虱，必为吾所效用也。鸷鸟逐雀，有袭人之怀，入人之室者，非出生也，后有惮也。

When raising an army, even the armor and helmets should breed lice and mites—they must all serve my purpose. A fierce bird chasing sparrows may suddenly pounce into a person's lap or enter their home—not because it is born with such intent, but because it fears something behind.

## 8

**武议:** 太公望年七十，屠牛朝歌，卖食盟津，过七十馀而主不听，人人谓之狂夫也。及遇文王，则提三万之众，一战而天下定，非武议安得此合也。故曰：'良马有策，远道可致；贤士有合，大道可明。'

Tai Gong Wang, at the age of seventy, slaughtered cattle in Chao Ge and sold food in Mengjin. After more than seventy years without a ruler heeding him, everyone regarded him as a madman. Only when he met King Wen did he lead thirty thousand troops, and with one battle the realm was stabilized—without martial wisdom, how could such a union have occurred? Therefore it is said: \"A fine horse needs a whip; distant roads can be reached. A worthy scholar must find the right ruler; only then can the great way become clear.\"

## 9

**武议:** 武王伐纣，师渡盟津，右旄左钺，死士三百，战士三万。纣之陈亿万，飞廉、恶来身先戟斧，陈开百里。武王不罢市民，兵不血刃，而克商诛纣，无祥异也，人事修不修而然也。

King Wu attacked Zhou, and his army crossed Mengjin. He held a banner on the right and an axe on the left, with three hundred fearless soldiers and thirty thousand warriors. Zhou's forces numbered in the millions; Feilian and Elai personally led halberds and axes, opening a battlefield stretching for one hundred li. King Wu did not mobilize the common people, his army did not spill blood, yet he conquered Shang and killed Zhou—this was no supernatural omen; it happened simply because human affairs were properly cultivated or neglected.

## 10

**武议:** 今世将考孤虚，占咸池，合龟兆，视吉凶，观星辰风云之变，欲以成胜立功，臣以为难。夫将者，上不制于天，下不制于地，中不制于人。故兵者，凶器也。争者，逆德也。将者，死官也。故不得已而用之。

Nowadays, generals examine the empty and occupied positions, divine by the Xianchi, consult turtle omens to determine auspiciousness or misfortune, observe changes in stars, winds, and clouds, hoping thereby to achieve victory and merit. I believe this is difficult. A general should not be constrained by heaven above, earth below, or people in between. Therefore, arms are instruments of violence. Strife is contrary to virtue. A general is an official of death. Therefore, it should be used only when absolutely necessary.

## 11

**武议:** 无天于上，无地于下，无主于后，无敌于前。一人之兵，如狼如虎，如风如雨，如雷如霆，震震冥冥，天下皆惊。

With no heaven above, no earth below, no commander behind, and no enemy ahead—An army led by one such person is like wolves and tigers, like wind and rain, like thunder and lightning—its presence shakes the heavens and startles all under heaven.

## 12

**武议:** 胜兵似水，夫水至柔弱者也，然所以触，丘陵必为之崩，无异也，性专而触诚也。今以莫邪之利，犀兕之坚，三军之众，有所奇正，则天下莫当其战矣。

A victorious army is like water; water is the softest and weakest, yet when it strikes, hills and mountains must collapse—this is no different. It happens because water's nature is focused and its impact sincere. Now, with the sharpness of Mo Ye, the toughness of rhinoceros and hippopotamus hides, and an army of three divisions employing both regular and unexpected tactics—there would be no force in the world that could withstand such a battle.

## 13

**武议:** 吴起与秦战，舍不平陇亩，朴嫩盖之，以蔽霜露，如此何也？不自高人故也。乞人之死不索尊，竭人之力不责礼，故古者甲胄之士不拜，示人无已烦也。夫烦人而欲乞其死，竭其力，自古至今，未尝闻矣。

Wu Qi, fighting against Qin, did not rest on level fields but slept in furrows and ditches, covering himself with branches to shield from frost and dew. Why was this so? Because he did not consider himself superior to others. To ask for a person's life without demanding respect, to exhaust their strength without expecting courtesy—thus in ancient times, soldiers in armor and helmets did not bow, showing others that they would not burden them. To trouble people yet demand their lives, to exhaust them and expect their strength—such a thing has never been heard of from ancient times until now.

## 14

**武议:** 将受命之日，忘其家，张军宿野忘其亲，援枹而鼓忘其身。吴起临战，左右进剑。起曰：'将专主旗鼓尔，临难决疑，挥兵指刃，此将事也。一剑之任，非将事也。'

On the day a general receives his orders, he forgets his home; when mustering troops and camping in the wild, he forgets his family; when raising the baton and beating the drum, he forgets himself. Wu Qi was about to go into battle, and those on either side presented him with swords. Qi said: \"A general's sole authority is over the banner and drum; to resolve doubts in times of crisis, to command troops and direct weapons—this is a general's duty. To wield a single sword is not the task of a general.\"

## 15

**武议:** 三军成行，一舍而后成三舍，三舍之馀，如决川源。望敌在前，因其所长而用之。敌白者垩之，赤者赭之。

When the three divisions of an army form ranks, they march one li and then complete three li. The remaining distance flows forward like a river breaking free from its source. Seeing the enemy ahead, use their strengths according to what they are good at. If the enemy is white in color, cover them with chalk; if red, stain them with ochre.

## 16

**武议:** 吴起与秦战未合，一夫不胜其勇，前获双首而还。吴起立命斩之。军吏谏曰：'此材士也，不可斩！' 起曰：'材士则是也，非吾令也。' 斩之。

Wu Qi was fighting against Qin before the forces had fully engaged when a single man, unable to contain his courage, rushed forward and returned having beheaded two enemies. Wu Qi immediately ordered him executed. A military official advised, \"This is a capable warrior; you cannot execute him!\" Qi said: \"He may be a capable man, but he did not follow my command.\" And so he was executed.

## 📖 类书引文

宋代《太平御览·兵部三十六·征伐下》引《尉缭子》佚文，对应本篇第9段武王伐纣之事，但内容有显著差异——多出河水逆流、左骖霆死等异象，可资校勘：

**太平御览引《尉缭子》曰：** 武王之伐纣也，河水逆流，左骖霆死，地方百里，战卒三万。纣之阵，起自黄鸟，至于赤斧，其间百里。武王不罢士民，兵不血刃，克殷诛纣，其人事然。

*The Taiping Yulan quotes the Wei Liaozi: When King Wu attacked Zhou, the river flowed backwards, the left trace-horse died from a thunderbolt, and the territory stretched a hundred li with 30,000 soldiers. Zhou's formation extended from Yellow Bird to Red Axe, covering a hundred li. King Wu did not exhaust his soldiers and people, and his troops did not stain their blades with blood, yet he conquered Yin and executed Zhou. This was simply a matter of human affairs.*

> 异文分析：武经七书本载"右旄左钺，死士三百，战士三万"及"飞廉、恶来身先戟斧"；太平御览本则作"河水逆流，左骖霆死""起自黄鸟，至于赤斧"。两相对照，武经七书本侧重人物（飞廉、恶来），太平御览本侧重地名（黄鸟、赤斧）与异象（河水逆流、左骖霆死）。值得注意的是，尉缭子本主张"无祥异也"，而太平御览引文却载有异象——或为唐代尉缭子文本已杂入谶纬之说，或为太平御览编修时从其他来源混入。

同书《兵部七十五·剑下》引《尉缭子》佚文，对应本篇第14段吴起临战左右进剑事：

**太平御览·剑下引《尉缭子》曰：** 吴越临战，左右进剑。

*The Taiping Yulan (Swords) quotes the Wei Liaozi: When Wu was about to engage Qi in battle, those around him presented a sword.*

同书《服用部四·盖》引《尉缭子》佚文，对应本篇第13段吴起"朴嫩盖之，以蔽霜露"：

**太平御览·盖引《尉缭子》曰：** 吴起与秦人战，仆嗽之盖，足以蔽霜露。

*The Taiping Yulan (Covers) quotes the Wei Liaozi: Wu Qi fought against the Qin people. The cover of his broken carriage was sufficient to shield him from frost and dew.*

> 异文：「仆嗽之盖」武经七书本作「朴嫩盖之」，文字略异而事同。

清代孙诒让《墨子闲诂》引《尉缭子》佚文，对应本篇第8段太公望事：

**墨子闲诂引《尉缭子》曰：** 太公望行年七十，屠牛朝歌，卖食棘津，遇七十馀主不听，人皆曰狂丈夫。

*Sun Yirang's Mozi Jiangu quotes the Wei Liaozi: Tai Gong Wang, at the age of seventy, slaughtered cattle in Chao Ge and sold food in Ji Jin. He encountered over seventy rulers who would not listen to him, and everyone called him a madman.*

> 异文：「卖食棘津」武经七书本作「卖食盟津」（地名不同）；「人皆曰狂丈夫」武经七书本作「人人谓之狂夫也」；「遇七十馀主不听」武经七书本作「过七十馀而主不听」。
