# 附录：银雀山汉简《尉缭子》辑录 · Yinqueshan Bamboo Slips of the Wei Liaozi

> 出土文献辑录：六篇与今本对应的汉简释文、零散佚文，以及今本与古本的差异对照。
>
> *An excavated-text compilation: transcriptions of the six Han-slip chapters corresponding to the received text, scattered fragments, and a comparison of the differences between the received and ancient texts.*

1972年山东临沂银雀山一号汉墓（西汉早期）出土大量竹简，其中既有与传世本对应的《尉缭子》六篇——〈兵谈〉〈攻权〉〈守权〉〈将理〉〈原官〉〈兵令〉——也有若干载有佚文的零散竹简。银雀山汉简是研究《尉缭子》文本演变最重要的出土材料：它证明今本（武经七书本）并非原书面貌，今本在流传中经历了明显的删并改造。

*In 1972, a large cache of bamboo slips was excavated from Tomb No. 1 at Yinqueshan, Linyi, Shandong (early Western Han). Among them were six chapters of the Wei Liaozi corresponding to the received text — Bing Tan, Gong Quan, Shou Quan, Jiang Li, Yuan Guan, and Bing Ling — as well as a number of scattered slips bearing fragments. The Yinqueshan slips are the single most important excavated material for the study of the Wei Liaozi's textual evolution: they prove that the received text (the Wujing Qishu recension) is not the original form of the work, which underwent pronounced abridgement and rearrangement in transmission.*

## 一、出土情况与结构差异 · Excavation and Structural Differences

- 汉简〈兵谈〉首句作"〔量土地肥〕硗而立邑建城"，今本作"量土地肥墝而立邑建城"；汉简多出"胜于丧纪、土功、市井"等治国维度，显示今本简化了政治经济论述。

  *The Han-slip "Bing Tan" opens "〔Measure the fertility of the land〕... and establish settlements and build cities," where the received text reads "measure the fertility of the land and establish settlements and build cities"; the slips add governance dimensions such as "prevailing over mourning rites, public works, and the marketplace," showing that the received text simplified the political-economic discussion.*

- 汉简〈兵令〉开篇强调"兵者，凶器、逆德；争者，事之〔末也〕"，今本《兵令上》首句为"兵者，凶器也；争者，逆德也"，删去了将战争定位为"末事"的评价。

  *The Han-slip "Bing Ling" opens by stressing "weapons are inauspicious instruments and the negation of virtue; contention is the last of affairs," whereas the received Bing Ling, Part I, reads "weapons are inauspicious instruments; contention is the negation of virtue," deleting the appraisal that locates war as "the last of affairs."*

- 汉简〈攻权〉作"兵以静固，以槫（专）胜"，今本作"兵以静胜"，军事哲学表述有异。

  *The Han-slip "Gong Quan" reads "an army is made firm by stillness and wins by concentration (zhuan)," where the received text reads "an army wins by stillness," differing in the formulation of military philosophy.*

### 1. 银雀山竹简〈兵谈〉 · Yinqueshan Slip "Bing Tan"

> 〔量土地肥〕硗而立邑建城。以城称地，以地〔称人，以人称粟；三相〕称也，故退可以守固，〔进可以〕战胜。战胜于外，福产于内。〔胜福相应，犹合符节，无异故也。〕……□□焚焚，产于无……大而不咷；关之，细而不咳。行广……□故王者，民之归之如流水，望……故曰明于〔禁舍开塞，则〕取天下若化。国贫者能富之，〔地不任者能任之，〕时不应者能应之。土广〔而任，则〕国不得无富；民众而制，则国不得无治。夫治且富之国，车不发〔轫〕，甲不出睪（橐），威〔制〕天下。故兵胜于朝廷，胜于丧纪，胜于土功，胜于市井。睪（橐）甲而胜，主胜也；阵而胜，主胜也；战胜，臣〔胜〕也；战再胜，当壹败。十万之师出，费日千金，□□□□，□□□，故百战百胜，不善者善……善者善者也。故善者成其刑（形）而民〔不知〕……胜而止，出……大矣，壹□而天下并。故患在百里之内者，不起一日之师；患在千里之内，不起一月之师；〔患在〕四海内，不起一岁之师。战胜其国，则攻其〔都；不胜其〕国，不攻其都。战胜天下，〔则攻其国〕；不胜天下，不攻其国。故名将而无家，绝苫（险）俞（逾）根（垠）而无主，左提鼓，右摅枹，而〔无〕生焉。故临生不为死，临死不为生。得带甲十万，〔驰〕车千乘，兵绝苫（险）俞（逾）根（垠），不□……怒，精（清）不可事以财。将之自治兆兆……〔心之生智〕，耳之生聪，目之生明。然使心狂者谁也？难得之货也。使耳聋者谁也？曰□□□□。〔使目盲〕者谁也？曰□泽好色也。……耳聋……〔兵之所〕及者，羊肠亦胜，锯齿亦胜，缘山入溪亦胜，方亦胜，圜（圆）亦胜，椭亦胜。兵重者如山……之压人，如云鲵（霓）复（覆）人。闭关辞交而廷中之，故□……□□□□所加兵者，令聚者不得〔散，散者不得〕聚；俯者不得迎（仰），迎（仰）者不得俯；左者不〔得右，右者不〕得左。知（智）士不给虑，甬（勇）士不……〔兵如丛〕木，弩如羊角，民人无……昌于于者胜成去。．治□

*After assessing the fertility of the land, one establishes settlements and builds cities. The city is weighed against the land, the land against the people, and the people against their grain; when these three are in balance, one can retreat and hold fast in defense, and advance and win in battle. Victory is won abroad, while good fortune is produced at home; victory and good fortune respond to each other like the two halves of a tally, without any discrepancy. … The great is not cramped; when closed, the small is not expansive. … Therefore the people of a true king return to him as water flows. … Hence it is said: one who understands what to prohibit and permit, what to open and close, will take the world as if by transformation. A poor state he can enrich; land that cannot bear burdens he can put to use; seasons that do not respond he can bring into harmony. When the territory is vast and put to use, the state cannot but grow rich; when the people are numerous and well ordered, the state cannot but be well governed. In a well-governed and wealthy state, the war chariots need not be set in motion, the armor need not be taken out of its cases, and its authority prevails over all under heaven. Hence the army is victorious in the court, victorious over mourning rites, victorious over public works, and victorious over the marketplace. To win with the armor still in its cases is the ruler's victory; to win after forming ranks is the ruler's victory; to win in battle is the ministers' victory; two victories in battle count as one defeat. When an army of a hundred thousand sets out, it costs a thousand pieces of gold a day. … Hence a hundred victories in a hundred battles are not the mark of one who is good; only one who is good is truly good. … Therefore one who is good completes his task while the people do not know it. … When the danger lies within a hundred li, one does not raise an army for a single day; when it lies within a thousand li, one does not raise an army for a single month; when it lies within the four seas, one does not raise an army for a single year. If one can defeat the enemy's state, then attack its capital; if one cannot defeat its state, do not attack its capital. If one can defeat the whole world, then attack its states; if one cannot defeat the whole world, do not attack its states. Hence the famous general has no home; he crosses precipices and overruns borders with no master above him, holding the drum on his left and the drumstick on his right, with no thought of life. Hence when facing life he does not act for the sake of death, and when facing death he does not act for the sake of life. … His anger … is incorruptible and cannot be swayed by wealth. … Hence the one who makes the mind mad—who is it? Rare goods. The one who makes the ear deaf—who is it? … The one who makes the eye blind—who is it? Fine silks and beautiful women. … Wherever the army reaches, it prevails on narrow paths like a sheep's intestine, on jagged ground like sawteeth, climbing mountains and entering valleys; in square formations, round ones, or oblong ones, it prevails. Heavy forces are like mountains pressing down on men, like clouds covering them. Closing the passes and cutting off intercourse while ordering affairs at court … it makes it so the enemy cannot disperse when gathered nor gather when dispersed; those bent cannot rise, those risen cannot bend; those on the left cannot move right, those on the right cannot move left. The wise are given no time to scheme, the brave no time to … Troops are like a clump of trees, crossbows like twisted ram's horns, and the people …*

> 对照要点：今本首句作"量土地肥墝而立邑建城"；汉简独有"胜于丧纪、胜于土功、胜于市井"——将战争胜负与丧礼、土木工程、市场治理并论，是"政兵一体"思想的直接体现，今本已删。

  *Comparison: the received text opens "measure the fertility of the land and establish settlements and build cities"; the slips uniquely preserve "prevailing over mourning rites, over public works, over the marketplace" — treating military victory alongside mourning rites, public works, and market governance, a direct expression of the "government and war as one" idea, deleted in the received text.*

### 2. 银雀山竹简〈攻权〉 · Yinqueshan Slip "Gong Quan"

> 〔兵以静〕固，以槫（专）胜。力分者弱，心疑者北。□□□故进退不稿（豪），从（纵）适（敌）不禽，将吏士卒，童（动）静如身。心疑必北，是故□……无尝试，发童（动）必蚤（早），亩凌而兵，无与战矣。〔夫将帅〕，心也；群下，支（肢）节也。其心童（动）……心童（动）疑，支（肢）节……下不节童（动），唯（虽）胜为幸，不壹不……〔□□者〕败，威立者胜。凡将死其道者……□□□威在志位，志位不代（忒），威乃……□爱者，将之成者也，是故兵不□□……以名信，信在孱兆。是故众聚不虚散，兵出不徒〔归〕，□□□□亡人，击适（敌）若卜（赴）溺者。囚险者毋战心，搕战毋胜兵，佻战毋全气。凡侠（挟）议（义）□□□□□起；争私结怨，贵以不得已。□□□起□适（敌）贵先。故事必当时，□必当□，〔故兵胜〕于朝廷，胜于丧纪，胜于土功，胜于市〔井〕……□败，曲胜者，其胜全，虽不曲胜，胜劝（权）……□□□以明吾胜也。．兵劝（权）

*An army is made firm by stillness and wins by concentration. Where strength is divided, it is weak; where minds are doubtful, it is defeated. … Hence advance and retreat are never rash, and one does not let the enemy go by indulging them; generals, officers, and soldiers move and rest as one body. When minds are doubtful, defeat is certain; therefore one never makes trial attacks, and when action is taken it must be early; … there is nothing to fight against such an army. The general is the heart, and the host below is the limbs and joints. When the heart moves … when the heart moves in doubt, the limbs and joints … if the subordinates do not move in due measure, even victory is a matter of luck; without singleness there is no … Those who are doubtful are defeated; those whose authority is established are victorious. … Authority resides in singleness of purpose; when purpose does not err, authority endures … One who is generous is the completion of a general, and therefore the army is never … trust is secured by reputation, and reputation lies in subtle omens. Hence when the multitude is gathered it does not scatter in vain, and when the army goes forth it does not return empty-handed; … seeking the enemy is like seeking a lost son, and striking the enemy is like plunging after a drowning man. Those hemmed in by danger have no will to fight, those who fight rashly have no army that wins, those who provoke battle have no full morale. Generally, when one fights upholding righteousness …; disputes over private gain and settled grudges are best entered upon only when unavoidable. … when battle is joined, it is best to strike first. Therefore affairs must accord with the time, and measures must accord with what is fitting; hence the army is victorious in the court, victorious over mourning rites, victorious over public works, victorious over the marketplace. … One who wins by a crooked route—his victory is complete only in part; even without a crooked victory, [he wins by] authority … to make plain our victory. · Bing Quan*

> 对照要点：今本作"兵以静胜"，汉简作"兵以静固，以专胜"，多出"专"（兵力集中）一义；汉简论"曲胜者，其胜全"——权谋之胜非全胜，今本仅存"战胜于外，备主于内"的功能性表述，思想重心发生偏移。

  *Comparison: the received text reads "an army wins by stillness," while the slips read "an army is made firm by stillness and wins by concentration (zhuan)," adding the notion of "concentration" (massing of force); the slips argue that "one who wins by a crooked route—his victory is complete only in part," meaning that a victory of stratagem is not a complete victory, whereas the received text preserves only the functional formulation "victory is won abroad, preparedness is maintained at home," shifting the center of gravity of the thought.*

### 3. 银雀山竹简〈守权〉 · Yinqueshan Slip "Shou Quan"

*When the strong crossbows and tough arrows within the outer city are exhausted … when the attackers' spirit has multiplied tenfold and a hundredfold while the defenders' spirit has not reached half, that is the gravest injury to defense. … he who defends does not go forth, and he who goes forth does not defend. The rule of defense: for every zhang of wall, so many men defend it … one man counts as ten, ten as a hundred, a hundred as ten thousand. … When the walls are strong and thick, the officers and men are well chosen, firewood and food are in due supply, the crossbows are strong and the arrows tough, and spears and halberds … such is the plan. The attacker can only be called a full force when it numbers ten thousand. If there is an army certain to come to its rescue, then there will be a people certain to fight; if there is no army certain to come to its rescue, then there will be no people certain to fight. … even the foolish man and the young woman exhaust the city and its resources … then defense is not to be prized. Rouse the heroic and the valiant, array stout armor and sharp weapons, strong crossbows and tough arrows in front, while the young and the broken follow behind; when fifty thousand men are truly resolved to rescue, close in on their rear, issue from the fortresses, strike the enemy from behind, cut off the passage of their grain, and respond from within and without …*

> 对照要点：汉简强调"必救之军"与"必战之民"的相互依存，及"中外相应"的协同防御，与今本《守权》的守城方略互有详略。

  *Comparison: the slips stress the mutual dependence of "an army certain to rescue" and "a people certain to fight," and the coordinated defense of "responding from within and without," which compare with the received Shou Quan's defensive strategy with differences of detail.*

### 5. 银雀山竹简〈原官〉 · Yinqueshan Slip "Yuan Guan"

> ……偿尊参会，移民之具也。均地分，节傅（赋）敛，□……臣主根也。刑赏明省，畏诛重奸，止奸……原，正（政）事之均也。……王之二术也。柤（俎）豆同利制天下……王者之德也。明礼常，霸者之……无事□，上无庆赏，民无狱讼，国无商贾，成王至正也；服奉下迵，成王至德也。

*… these are the instruments for shifting the people. Equalize the division of land, moderate the levying of taxes … the root of minister and sovereign. When punishments and rewards are clear and sparing, when men dread execution and felony is severely dealt with, wrongdoing is stopped … such is the evenness of government affairs. … the two arts of the king. With the sacrificial vessels sharing the benefits, one governs the world … such is the virtue of the king. Clarifying the constant norms of ritual … of the hegemon. … when there is no business left to be done, when the sovereign above grants no rewards for merit, the people have no lawsuits, and the state has no merchants — this is the perfect uprightness of the completed king; when clothing and support flow down to all, this is the perfect virtue of the completed king.*

> 对照要点：汉简〈原官〉涉及均地分、节赋敛、明礼常等制度内容，印证原书包含系统的国家制度设计，今本此篇已大为简化。

  *Comparison: the Han-slip "Yuan Guan" concerns institutional matters such as equalizing land division, moderating taxation, and clarifying the norms of ritual, confirming that the original work contained systematic state-institutional design, of which the received chapter is greatly simplified.*

### 6. 银雀山竹简〈兵令〉 · Yinqueshan Slip "Bing Ling"

## 五、佚失内容的分类与主题 · Categories and Themes of the Lost Content

综合现存佚文，可归纳四类主题：

*Drawing together the extant fragments, four thematic categories may be distinguished:*

### 1. 国家制度（反映儒法融合）· State Institutions (reflecting Confucian–Legalist fusion)

- 宅制：《初学记》引"天子宅千亩，诸侯百亩，大夫以下里舍九亩"——等级空间制度
  *Dwelling regulations: the Chuxue Ji quotes "the Son of Heaven's residence is a thousand mu, the feudal lords' a hundred mu, below the grand officers nine mu per hamlet dwelling" — a rank-based spatial system.*
- 冠冕礼制：《太平御览》《隋书》引"天子玄冠玄缨，诸侯素冠素缨"——等级规范
  *Cap-and-tassel ritual: the Taiping Yulan and Suishu quote "the Son of Heaven wears a dark cap with dark tassels, the feudal lords plain caps with plain tassels" — rank norms.*

### 2. 战争伦理（有限战争论）· Ethics of War (the doctrine of limited war)

- "不杀夭胎，不脍不成之材"——军事生态伦理（道家生态观）
  *"Do not kill the unborn, do not cut unripe timber" — a martial-ecological ethic (Daoist ecological outlook).*
- "战胜其国，则攻其都；不胜其国，不攻其都"——有限战争论
  *"If one can defeat the enemy's state, then attack its capital; if one cannot defeat its state, do not attack its capital" — the doctrine of limited war.*

### 3. 战略哲学 · Strategic Philosophy

- "小鱼渔渊，中鱼渔国，大鱼渔天下"——战略层级理论
  *"The small fish fishes the deep waters, the middle fish the state, the great fish the world" — a tiered theory of strategy.*
- "去备彻威而胜"——反常制胜思维
  *"Set aside preparations and lay down authority, yet win" — a counterintuitive conception of victory.*

### 4. 军事技术 · Military Technology

- "无冲笼而攻"——攻城禁忌
  *"Attack without siege towers or wagons" — a prohibition in siege warfare.*
- "津梁未发，要塞未修"——防御漏洞
  *"The fords and bridges are not yet opened, the strategic points not yet repaired" — defensive vulnerabilities.*

这些内容表明《尉缭子》原书是融合兵、法、儒、道的综合性政论，远超今本"兵技巧"范畴。

*These contents show that the original Wei Liaozi was a comprehensive political treatise fusing Military, Legalist, Confucian, and Daoist strands, far exceeding the received text's category of "military technique."*

## 六、佚失原因与文本嬗变 · Causes of Loss and Textual Transformation

### 思想史动因 · Intellectual-Historical Causes

战国至秦汉兵学从"政兵一体"转向"军事专业化"，宋明以后更重实用战术，导致伦理、礼制等内容被视为"无用之文"而遭淘汰。

*From the Warring States through the Qin–Han, military learning shifted from "government and war as one" toward "military professionalization"; from the Song–Ming onward, practical tactics were emphasized still more, so that ethical and ritual content came to be regarded as "useless writing" and was discarded.*

## 七、学术价值再认识 · Reassessing the Scholarly Value

1. **思想史意义**：佚文证明尉缭思想是战国黄老学派的实践——"不杀夭胎"呼应《淮南子》兵道观，"宅制"体现《管子》空间政治学。今本《尉缭子》实为宋代兵学实用主义筛选的产物，银雀山佚文及类书引文共同证明原书实为以兵论政的"帝王全书"，其佚失部分正是战国兵学与百家思想交融的关键环节。

   *Significance for intellectual history: the fragments prove that Wei Liao's thought was a practical expression of the Warring States Huang-Lao school — "do not kill the unborn" echoes the military Daoism of the Huainanzi, and the dwelling regulations embody the spatial politics of the Guanzi. The received Wei Liaozi is in fact the product of Song-dynasty military pragmatism's filtering; the Yinqueshan fragments and the encyclopedia quotations together show that the original work was a "complete book for emperors" discussing politics through war, and that its lost portions are precisely a key link in the fusion of Warring States military learning with the various schools.*

2. **军事制度史价值**：冠缨制反映战国军阶萌芽；宅制揭示"军功授田"的配套政策。

   *Value for military-institutional history: the cap-and-tassel system reflects the incipient military ranks of the Warring States; the dwelling regulations reveal the supporting policy of "granting land for military merit."*

> 研究启示：未来研究需跳出"武经七书"框架，在诸子整合视角下重构尉缭思想。

  *Research implication: future study must step outside the framework of the "Seven Military Classics" and reconstruct Wei Liao's thought from an integrated, all-schools perspective.*
